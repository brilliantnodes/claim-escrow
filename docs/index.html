ClaimEscrow

A safe way to buy and sell bankruptcy claims.

When a company goes bust, the people it owes money to can sell their right to be paid (a "claim") for cash today. ClaimEscrow holds the buyer's money and pays the seller only when the official public list of the company's debts (the claims register) shows the sale went through and how much the claim is worth. Bankruptcy claims are one part of litigation funding, a market of about $25bn in 2025 (Custom Market Insights).

The whole price sits in escrow. Validators read who holds the claim, its status and its allowed amount on the register page; plain code decides every amount. Nothing moves on one look: a state counts once it is seen twice, a set time apart.

The trust problem

A creditor of a bankrupt company sells its claim for cash now. The price is a share of the claim, but the claim's worth is decided later, when it is allowed in full, cut or rejected. Today either the buyer pays in full and chases a refund if the claim is cut, or holds part back and the seller trusts it to pay later. The facts that decide the money are public, on the claims register, but on a page made for people.

How it works

1. Offer and baseline

The seller freezes the terms: buyer, both names as the register shows them, the claim's page (on a host frozen at deployment), price, upfront share, deadlines. A first reading must show the seller holding the claim.

2. Accept

The buyer pays exactly the price and the digest of the terms. The money is in escrow.

3. Transfer

When the register shows the buyer as holder twice, a set time apart, the upfront goes to the seller (if the status then is unclear, once the claim shows live with the buyer twice).

4. Decision

Allowed, disallowed or withdrawn, seen twice: code pays the price for the allowed amount, or refunds the buyer. A withdrawal pays the seller only if the register first showed the buyer holding the claim live. Anything unclear moves nothing.

5. Deadlines

No transfer in time refunds the buyer; no decision by the long stop splits what is left by an agreed share, unless the seller was seen holding the claim again twice. Readings count at their own time stamp; deadlines take effect 30 minutes late. No model needed.

6. Credits

Every payout is a credit each side withdraws itself. Once every withdrawal has finalized, the contract's balance equals what is locked plus what is credited; each run below was checked that way.
